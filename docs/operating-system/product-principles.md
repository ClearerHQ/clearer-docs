# The Clearer Product Principles

The Constitution defines what Clearer believes.

The Product Principles define how those beliefs become products.

Every feature, interaction and design decision should be evaluated against these principles before it is built.

If a feature conflicts with these principles, the feature changes - not the principles.

---

## 1. Reduce Cognitive Load

### Principle

Every interaction should make someone's digital life feel simpler, not more complicated.

### Why it matters

People already have enough decisions to make.

Clearer exists to reduce mental effort, not add to it.

### What this means

✅ Reduce unnecessary choices.

✅ Prefer sensible defaults.

✅ Keep interfaces calm and uncluttered.

✅ Make the next action obvious.

❌ Add complexity for flexibility's sake.

❌ Require users to learn how the product works.

❌ Hide important actions behind unnecessary menus.

### Example

Instead of asking someone to organise fifty emails into folders, Clearer groups similar emails together and lets them make one simple decision.

---

## 2. AI Suggests. Humans Decide.

### Principle

Artificial intelligence exists to help people make better decisions.

It should never quietly make important decisions on their behalf.

### Why it matters

People trust products they understand.

They distrust products that act without permission.

### What this means

✅ Recommend emails to archive.

✅ Suggest duplicate photos.

✅ Learn user preferences over time.

❌ Automatically delete content.

❌ Permanently remove files without review.

❌ Make irreversible decisions silently.

### Example

Instead of saying:

"Delete these 53 emails."

Clearer says:

"You've archived newsletters like these 87 times before.

Would you like to archive these as well?"

---

## 3. Trust Is a Feature.

### Principle

Trust is not something users give us.

It is something we earn through every interaction.

### Why it matters

People are trusting Clearer with parts of their digital life.

That trust must never be taken for granted.

### What this means

✅ Explain why permissions are needed.

✅ Explain why recommendations are made.

✅ Make actions reversible wherever possible.

✅ Be transparent about how AI works.

❌ Hide important information.

❌ Surprise users.

❌ Optimise for engagement at the expense of confidence.

### Example

Instead of asking for inbox access without context, Clearer explains exactly why access is needed, what data is used, what is never stored, and how the user remains in control.

---

## 4. Explain Every Recommendation

### Principle

People should always understand why Clearer is making a recommendation.

Recommendations should build confidence, not create uncertainty.

### Why it matters

Artificial intelligence should feel like a trusted assistant, not a black box.

When people understand why something has been suggested, they are far more likely to trust both the recommendation and the product.

### What this means

✅ Explain why an email, photo or file has been recommended.

✅ Show patterns the user has previously established.

✅ Keep explanations simple and easy to understand.

❌ Ask users to "trust the AI."

❌ Make recommendations without context.

❌ Hide the reasoning behind important decisions.

### Example

Instead of saying:

"Archive these emails."

Clearer says:

"You've archived newsletters from this sender 42 times before. These look similar."

---

## 5. Default to Reversible Actions

### Principle

People should feel confident exploring Clearer without worrying about making irreversible mistakes.

### Why it matters

Fear creates hesitation.

Confidence creates progress.

When people know they can undo an action, they're far more willing to make decisions.

### What this means

✅ Archive before deleting where possible.

✅ Provide an Undo option after important actions.

✅ Keep recently removed items recoverable.

✅ Clearly communicate when something becomes permanent.

❌ Permanently delete content without confirmation.

❌ Hide recovery options.

❌ Make users fear pressing a button.

### Example

After archiving a group of emails, Clearer displays:

"Archived 24 emails. Undo?"

The user remains in control at every step.

---

## 6. Build Habits, Not Dependence

### Principle

Clearer should help people develop healthy digital habits, not encourage them to spend more time inside the app.

### Why it matters

Many products measure success by engagement.

We measure success by confidence.

The best experience is one where people accomplish what they came to do and move on with their day.

### What this means

✅ Encourage short, focused sessions.

✅ Celebrate consistency over intensity.

✅ Make progress visible.

✅ Respect when someone wants to leave.

❌ Create artificial streaks.

❌ Send unnecessary reminders.

❌ Optimise for screen time.

### Example

Instead of encouraging people to spend longer in the app, Clearer celebrates:

"Five minutes today. Your inbox is becoming clearer."

The session ends there.

The goal is progress, not retention.

---

## 7. Celebrate Clarity, Not Deletion

### Principle

Success is not measured by how much content disappears.

It is measured by how much lighter people feel afterwards.

### Why it matters

Deleting thousands of emails or photos is not the goal.

Helping people regain confidence in their digital life is.

The feeling matters more than the number.

### What this means

✅ Celebrate meaningful progress.

✅ Show people the positive impact of their decisions.

✅ Focus on clarity rather than statistics.

❌ Reward deleting content for its own sake.

❌ Encourage people to remove things they might later regret.

❌ Treat bigger numbers as better outcomes.

### Example

Instead of saying:

"You deleted 2,431 emails."

Clearer says:

"Your inbox is now easier to manage."

The achievement isn't the deletion.

The achievement is the clarity.

---

## 8. Every Interaction Should Feel Calm

### Principle

Clearer should reduce stress, not contribute to it.

Every interaction should feel predictable, respectful and unhurried.

### Why it matters

Digital clutter is already overwhelming.

The product designed to solve that problem should never create additional pressure.

Calm is part of the experience - not just the visual design.

### What this means

✅ Use clear, reassuring language.

✅ Keep interfaces simple.

✅ Give people time to make decisions.

✅ Celebrate progress quietly.

❌ Use countdowns or urgency.

❌ Interrupt people unnecessarily.

❌ Create anxiety through notifications or messaging.

### Example

Instead of saying:

"Your inbox needs attention."

Clearer says:

"When you're ready, we've identified a few emails you may want to review."

The difference is subtle.

The feeling is completely different.

---

## 9. Respect People's Attention

### Principle

Attention is one of the most valuable things people have.

Clearer should protect it, not compete for it.

### Why it matters

Many digital products are designed to capture attention.

Clearer exists to give it back.

Every notification, prompt and interruption should earn its place.

### What this means

✅ Send notifications only when they provide genuine value.

✅ Keep reminders optional.

✅ Let people choose how and when they engage.

✅ Finish tasks efficiently.

❌ Interrupt people for engagement.

❌ Send notifications simply to increase usage.

❌ Create fear of missing out.

### Example

Instead of sending a daily reminder to clean your inbox, Clearer waits until there is something genuinely worth reviewing and only notifies you if you've chosen to receive those notifications.

---

## 10. Solve One Problem Well

### Principle

Focus creates better products.

We would rather solve one problem exceptionally well than solve ten problems poorly.

### Why it matters

Every new feature introduces complexity.

By staying focused, we build products that are easier to understand, easier to trust and easier to use.

Scope is one of the biggest risks to product quality.

### What this means

✅ Prioritise depth over breadth.

✅ Finish features before starting new ones.

✅ Build only what clearly supports our mission.

❌ Add features simply because competitors have them.

❌ Sacrifice simplicity in pursuit of completeness.

❌ Expand into new problems before solving existing ones.

### Example

Rather than launching email, photos, files and notes all at once, Clearer focuses on making inbox management genuinely useful before expanding into other areas of digital life.

---

## 11. Progress Over Perfection

### Principle

Small, consistent improvements create lasting change.

Perfection is not the goal.

Progress is.

### Why it matters

People don't become organised overnight.

They become organised by making small, repeatable decisions over time.

The product should encourage progress without making people feel like they've fallen behind.

### What this means

✅ Celebrate small wins.

✅ Encourage consistency.

✅ Make improvement visible.

✅ Allow people to continue where they left off.

❌ Shame people for inactivity.

❌ Create unrealistic expectations.

❌ Make perfection feel like the objective.

### Example

Instead of saying:

"You still have 12,000 unread emails."

Clearer says:

"You've reduced your inbox by 600 emails this month."

The focus is on the progress already made—not the work that remains.

---

## 12. Consistency Creates Confidence

### Principle

People should always know what to expect when using Clearer.

Consistency builds familiarity.

Familiarity builds confidence.

### Why it matters

When interactions behave consistently, people spend less time thinking about the interface and more time making confident decisions.

Predictability reduces cognitive load.

### What this means

✅ Keep interactions consistent across the product.

✅ Use familiar patterns wherever possible.

✅ Write in a consistent tone of voice.

✅ Make similar actions behave the same way.

❌ Introduce unnecessary variation.

❌ Change behaviour without good reason.

❌ Surprise users with inconsistent experiences.

### Example

Whether someone is reviewing emails, photos or files, the interaction model remains familiar.

The content changes.

The experience does not.

---

# Closing Thoughts

Every feature we build should make Clearer feel more like Clearer.

These principles are not suggestions.

They are the standards by which we evaluate our work.

Features will evolve.

Technology will change.

These principles are intended to endure.

When faced with difficult product decisions, we return to them.

**If a feature conflicts with these principles, the feature changes - not the principles.**
