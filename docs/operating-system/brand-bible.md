# The Clearer Brand Bible

The Constitution defines why Clearer exists.

The Product Principles define how Clearer behaves.

The Design Language defines how Clearer should feel.

The Brand Bible defines how Clearer communicates.

Every word we write should build trust.

Whether someone is reading a notification, an onboarding screen or a support article, it should always feel unmistakably like Clearer.

If our words create confusion, unnecessary urgency or uncertainty, they need rewriting.

---

## 1. Our Personality

If Clearer were a person, they would be:

- Calm.
- Thoughtful.
- Honest.
- Reassuring.
- Patient.
- Quietly confident.
- Helpful.
- Curious.

We are never:

- Loud.
- Pushy.
- Manipulative.
- Overly technical.
- Condescending.
- Corporate.
- Attention-seeking.

Our personality should remain consistent regardless of where people encounter us.

---

## 2. Our Voice

Our voice is consistent.

It does not change depending on the feature or platform.

We write:

- Clearly.
- Simply.
- Honestly.
- Directly.
- With empathy.

We avoid unnecessary jargon.

We prefer everyday language over technical language.

We explain.

We never impress for the sake of it.

---

## 3. Our Tone

Our voice remains consistent.

Our tone adapts to the situation.

When celebrating progress - Be encouraging.

When explaining something - Be patient.

When asking for permissions - Be transparent.

When something goes wrong - Be reassuring.

When giving advice - Be confident, but never demanding.

The user's emotional state should always influence our tone.

---

## 4. Writing Principles

Every sentence should make life feel a little clearer.

When writing for Clearer, we:

- Prefer clarity over cleverness.
- Explain before we persuade.
- Keep sentences short.
- Remove unnecessary words.
- Use plain English.
- Write with confidence, not certainty.
- Respect the reader's attention.

Every word should earn its place.

If removing a sentence improves understanding, remove it.

---

## 5. Words We Use

Certain ideas appear throughout Clearer because they reinforce our purpose.

We naturally use words like:

- Clear
- Clarity
- Calm
- Focus
- Lighter
- Progress
- Review
- Organise
- Confident
- Thoughtful
- Simple

These words reinforce how we want people to feel.

We don't force them into every sentence.

We use them naturally and consistently.

---

## 6. Words We Avoid

Some words immediately create the wrong feeling.

Where possible, we avoid language that creates urgency, pressure or unnecessary complexity.

Examples include:

- Urgent
- Immediately
- Hurry
- Amazing
- Revolutionary
- Game-changing
- Magic
- AI-powered
- Optimise
- Leverage
- Synergy

We avoid unnecessary marketing language.

We avoid technical jargon when simpler language exists.

**Our goal is understanding - not impression.**

---

## 7. Naming Things

Names should be simple, descriptive and easy to remember.

We prefer everyday language over creative language.

Good examples:

- Inbox Reset
- Review Session
- Suggestions
- Archive
- Recently Removed

Less suitable examples:

- Inbox Optimiser
- AI Cleanup Engine
- Productivity Hub

People should understand a feature before they use it.

Names should reduce uncertainty, not increase it.

---

## 8. Writing for the Product

Product copy should help people make confident decisions.

It should never create unnecessary pressure.

### Buttons

Use clear action-oriented labels.

Good:

- Archive
- Review
- Continue
- Try Again

Avoid:

- Let's Go!
- Optimise
- Supercharge

### Success Messages

Celebrate progress quietly.

Good: "Your inbox is a little clearer."

Avoid: "Congratulations! You smashed it!"

### Error Messages

Explain what happened.

Explain what happens next.

Reassure people that nothing unexpected has happened.

Good: "We couldn't connect to your inbox. Nothing has changed. Please try again."

Avoid: "Authentication failed."

### Permissions

Always explain why we're asking.

People should understand the benefit before granting access.

Never request permissions without context.

---

## 9. Writing for Marketing

Marketing should educate before it persuades.

We don't create urgency.

We don't exaggerate.

We don't promise perfection.

We explain the problem.

We explain our approach.

We let people decide.

### We avoid

- Fear of missing out.
- Artificial scarcity.
- Clickbait.
- Overpromising.

### We prefer

- Honest stories.
- Practical examples.
- Clear explanations.
- Simple language.

Our marketing should feel like an extension of the product.

---

## 10. Examples

### Example 1

Instead of:

"You have 5,328 unread emails!"

We write:

"Ready for five minutes?

Let's make your inbox a little clearer."


### Example 2

Instead of:

"Optimise your productivity."

We write:

"Small decisions.

A clearer inbox."


### Example 3

Instead of:

"AI has categorised your emails."

We write:

"We've found a few emails that look similar based on how you've organised your inbox before."


### Example 4

Instead of:

"Authentication failed."

We write:

"We couldn't connect to your inbox.

Nothing has changed.

Please try again."

---

## 11. Closing Thoughts

Words shape how people experience products.

Every sentence we write should reduce uncertainty.

Every explanation should build trust.

Every message should leave people feeling calmer than before.

We don't write to sound clever.

We write to make things clearer.

If a sentence creates confusion, rewrite it.

If a sentence creates confidence, keep it.

**Clear words.**

**Clear thinking.**

**A clearer digital life.**
